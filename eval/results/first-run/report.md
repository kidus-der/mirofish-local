## The Unlikely Coalition Against the Tariff

The Unlikely Coalition Against the Tariff

As the July 15 council vote approaches, a surprising alliance has coalesced to block the Harbor Vale Water Authority's 9 percent tariff plan. This coalition is not built on shared ideologies but on a singular, non-negotiable demand: job guarantees for the 200 maintenance positions currently at risk. The convergence of labor unions, city officials, and community leaders signals a high-stakes political standoff that could reshape the city's infrastructure policy.

**The Core Demand: Jobs Over Rate Hikes**

The driving force behind this unity is the fear that the tariff increase will come at the direct expense of essential workers. Samuel Adeyemi, who leads the Water Workers Union, articulates the group's primary stance clearly:

> "The Water Workers Union opposes the 9 percent tariff plan without job guarantees for the 200 maintenance positions."

This sentiment is echoed across the coalition. The Tenants Alliance of Harbor Vale, led by Priya Raman, has explicitly backed the union's position, viewing the issue through the lens of worker security:

> "The Tenants Alliance of Harbor Vale supports the Water Workers Union's demand for job guarantees for the 200 maintenance positions."

Even within the city council, the opposition has solidified. Council member Daniel Oyelaran has joined the fray, opposing the plan specifically because it lacks these necessary protections for the workforce.

**A Unified Front Across Diverse Groups**

The coalition's strength lies in its cross-sector nature. It is not just a union-led protest; it is a broader movement involving:

- **The Water Workers Union**: Leading the charge on job security.
- **The Tenants Alliance of Harbor Vale**: Supporting the union and opposing rate hikes that jeopardize livelihoods.
- **City Council Members**: Specifically Daniel Oyelaran, who opposes the plan without job guarantees.
- **Community Leaders**: Including Ilse Varga, a community organizer in the Eastside district, who opposes the revision plan proposed by the Harbor Vale Water Authority.

The support extends even to the local press, which seems to align with the coalition's goals. Lena Fischer of *The Harbor Vale Courier* supports the call for transparency and accountability, a stance that resonates with the coalition's broader demand for fair treatment:

> "The Tenants Alliance of Harbor Vale supports the stance of The Harbor Vale Courier regarding job guarantees and opposing rate hikes that jeopardize essential workers' livelihoods."

**The Threat of Action**

The stakes have been raised significantly. The Water Workers Union has made it clear that the status quo is not an option if their demands are ignored. They have issued a stark warning regarding the upcoming vote:

> "The Water Workers Union stands ready to strike if the city council votes for the 9 percent tariff plan without a guaranteed guarantee for our 200 maintenance positions."

This threat of a strike adds a layer of high-stakes pressure to the political standoff, transforming the debate from a mere policy discussion into a potential crisis of essential services. The coalition's arguments are no longer just about the percentage of the tariff; they are about the fundamental stability of the city's infrastructure workforce.

**Arguments Dominating Public Debate**

As the vote approaches, the public debate is dominated by two central arguments:

1.  **Equity and Fairness**: The coalition argues that a rate hike without job guarantees is unacceptable. They frame the issue as a choice between cutting costs for consumers and maintaining a stable, employed workforce.
2.  **Transparency and Accountability**: Related to the broader corruption scandal involving Brightline Consulting, the coalition insists on full transparency. Lena Fischer notes the demand to "publish every contract and demands full accountability before the July 15th vote," linking the tariff issue to broader governance failures.

The coalition's unified message is one of resistance against a plan they view as predatory and unstable. As the July 15 deadline looms, the question remains whether the city council will cave to this unified front or proceed with a vote that could trigger a widespread strike.
