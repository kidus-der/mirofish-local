# The Harbor Vale Water Dispute: Briefing File

## Background

Harbor Vale is a coastal city of 240,000 people. Its drinking water is supplied by the Harbor Vale Water Authority, usually called HVWA. In January, HVWA chief executive Tomas Reyes announced a plan to raise household water tariffs by 18 percent to pay for replacing the Kessler Reservoir pipeline, which was built in 1961.

Mayor Ilse Varga backed the plan at first. In a statement to the city council she said the pipeline was "a failure waiting to happen". Council member Daniel Oyelaran, who chairs the council's finance committee, opposed the rise and argued that low-income households would carry most of the burden.

## Early opposition

The Tenants Alliance of Harbor Vale, led by organizer Priya Raman, started a petition against the tariff in February. Within three weeks the petition had 31,000 signatures. Raman said that renters in the Eastside district already pay more per litre than homeowners because of old shared meters.

The Harbor Vale Courier, the city's largest newspaper, published an investigation by reporter Lena Fischer. Fischer reported that HVWA had paid 4.2 million dollars to Brightline Consulting for a pipeline study. Brightline Consulting is owned by Marco Reyes, the brother of the HVWA chief executive.

## The scandal widens

After the Courier story, Council member Oyelaran asked the state auditor, Grace Lindqvist, to examine the contract. Lindqvist opened a formal audit of HVWA in March.

Tomas Reyes denied any wrongdoing at first. On 28 March he resigned as chief executive of the Authority. The HVWA board appointed its chief engineer, Nadia Okafor, as acting chief executive. Okafor said she would publish every pipeline contract.

Mayor Varga withdrew her support for the 18 percent increase on 2 April and called for a pause. The mayor said public trust had to be rebuilt before any new charge.

## A new proposal

In May, Okafor presented a revised plan. The tariff rise was cut from 18 percent to 9 percent, with a discount for households on income support. The remaining cost would be covered by a 60 million dollar loan from the Coastal Infrastructure Bank.

The Water Workers Union, led by Samuel Adeyemi, had supported the original plan because it protected maintenance jobs. Adeyemi turned against the revised plan, saying the smaller rise would force HVWA to cut 200 maintenance positions.

Priya Raman welcomed the income-support discount but said the Tenants Alliance would keep campaigning until shared meters in Eastside were replaced.

## Where things stand

The audit by Lindqvist found in June that the Brightline contract had broken procurement rules, although it found no evidence that Tomas Reyes personally profited. The city council will vote on the 9 percent plan on 15 July. Daniel Oyelaran now says he will support the revised plan. The Water Workers Union has threatened a strike if the plan passes without a guarantee on jobs.
